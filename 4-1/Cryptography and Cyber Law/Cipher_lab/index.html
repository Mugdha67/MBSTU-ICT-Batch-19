Cipher Classification & Theory

Classical ciphers are divided into substitution and transposition. Substitution ciphers replace plaintext symbols with ciphertext symbols.

Monoalphabetic Poly alphabetic Playfair (digraph) Hill (matrix)

Monoalphabetic: fixed substitution (A→Z).
Playfair: encrypts pairs of letters using a 5×5 matrix (I/J merged).
Hill cipher: uses linear algebra (matrix multiplication) for encryption/decryption.

All three are symmetric-key ciphers. The key defines the substitution or matrix.

Monoalphabetic

Simple substitution, 26! keys. Vulnerable to frequency analysis.

Playfair

Digraph substitution, 5×5 matrix. Harder to crack than monoalphabetic.

Hill (2×2)

Matrix multiplication mod 26. Requires invertible key matrix.

Playfair Cipher
